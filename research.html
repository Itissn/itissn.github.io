Research Overview

My research focuses on automated algorithm design for large-scale vehicle routing problems. In particular, I study how evolutionary computation, genetic programming, guided local search, curriculum learning, behaviour niching, and surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation can improve the scalability and robustness of routing solvers.

The central goal of my PhD research is to develop learning-based and evolutionary methods that can automatically design, adapt, and improve heuristic search strategies for large-scale vehicle routing problems.

Research Themes

Large-Scale Vehicle Routing Problems

Vehicle routing problems become particularly challenging at large scales, where the number of customers, the size of the search space, and practical computational limits make efficient heuristic search essential.

Instance Space Analysis

I use instance space analysis to study the relationship between vehicle routing instance features and constructive heuristic performance. This helps identify where different heuristics succeed or fail and provides a foundation for understanding learned search guidance.

Genetic Programming Guided Local Search

I study genetic programming guided local search, where genetic programming automatically designs utility functions for guided local search. These utility functions can use instance, solution, and edge-level features to guide the search process.

Curriculum Learning for GPGLS

I investigate curriculum learning strategies for GPGLS, including predefined curriculum learning and adaptive curriculum learning. These methods aim to improve training efficiency by controlling the order and difficulty of training instances.

Dynamic Curriculum Learning

I develop dynamic curriculum learning methods that estimate instance difficulty online from solver feedback, such as observed optimality gaps. This allows the curriculum process to adapt to the evolving competence of the solver during training.

Behaviour Niching

I study behaviour-based niching for genetic programming in GPGLS. This line of work investigates how behavioural diversity among GP trees can be maintained during evolution and how unnecessary tree complexity can be controlled.

Surrogate-Assisted GPGLS

I am also exploring surrogate-assisted GPGLS, where surrogate models are used to reduce expensive fitness evaluations and improve the efficiency of GP-based solver training.

Research Direction

Overall, my work aims to advance GPGLS for large-scale vehicle routing problems through instance-space understanding, GP-based search guidance, curriculum learning, dynamic curriculum learning, behaviour niching, and surrogate-assisted training.
