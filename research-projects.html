Research & Projects

My research focuses on automated algorithm design for large-scale vehicle routing problems. My projects investigate how evolutionary computation, genetic programming, guided local search, curriculum learning, behaviour niching, and surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation can improve the efficiency, robustness, and scalability of routing solvers.

Research Roadmap

1. Instance Space Analysis for Large-Scale Vehicle Routing Problems

This project analyses the relationship between vehicle routing instance features and the performance of constructive heuristics. It aims to identify where different heuristics succeed or fail and to provide an instance-space understanding of large-scale vehicle routing problems.

Status: submitted to PPSN.

2. Genetic Programming Guided Local Search for LSVRPs

This project develops GPGLS, where genetic programming automatically designs utility functions for guided local search. The evolved utility functions use instance, solution, and edge-level features to guide the search process.

Status: published in PPSN.

3. Curriculum Learning and Adaptive Curriculum Learning in GPGLS

This project studies curriculum-based training strategies for GPGLS. It first applies predefined curriculum learning and then develops adaptive strategies that use training performance to control curriculum transitions and reduce training generations.

Status: CL-GPGLS published in CEC; ACL-GPGLS published in EvoStar.

4. Dynamic Curriculum Learning for GPGLS

This project develops an online dynamic curriculum learning framework for GPGLS. It estimates instance difficulty from solver feedback, such as observed optimality gaps, and uses the estimated difficulty to guide curriculum selection under limited computational budgets.

Status: submitted to PPSN.

5. Behaviour Niching in GPGLS

This ongoing project investigates behaviour-based niching for GP trees. It studies how behavioural diversity can be maintained during evolution and how smaller trees can be preferred among behaviourally similar individuals to improve search robustness and control unnecessary complexity.

Status: research in progress.

6. Surrogate-Assisted GPGLS

This planned project studies how surrogate models can reduce expensive GPGLS fitness evaluations and improve training efficiency. It may involve GP individual characterisation, surrogate-assisted selection, and experimental analysis under limited computational budgets.

Status: research plan in preparation.

Overall Research Direction

Overall, these projects form a coherent research programme for improving GPGLS on large-scale vehicle routing problems, moving from instance-space understanding and GP-based search guidance to curriculum learning, dynamic curriculum learning, behaviour niching, and surrogate-assisted training.
